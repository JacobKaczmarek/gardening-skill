# Peace Lily

*Spathiphyllum wallisii* — beginner-friendly tropical; the #1 killer is root rot from waterlogged soil, not underwatering — dramatic wilting is a thirst signal, not a death sentence.

## Care at a glance

- **Light:** low to medium indirect; 1,000–2,500 lux for healthy foliage, 3,000+ lux for reliable blooming. North or east window works well. Min 1,000 lux. Too much: pale washed-out leaves, scorched spathe edges. Too little: very dark green leaves, no spathes.
- **Water:** every 7 days / 12 days winter; keep soil evenly moist but never soggy — water when the top 2–3 cm dries slightly. Plant wilts dramatically when thirsty but recovers in 1–2 hours after watering. Fluoride and chlorine accumulate and cause brown tips; use filtered water or leave tap water open overnight.
- **Temperature:** ideal 18–27°C; min survivable 10°C; below 13°C causes leaf damage. Avoid cold draughts, unheated windows, and AC vents. Cold damage: blackened mushy tissue.
- **Humidity:** high, 50–80%. Below 40% causes persistent brown leaf tips. Humidifier or bathroom placement is most effective. No misting — it raises humidity for minutes then fosters fungal spotting.
- **Soil:** 60% peat-based mix + 25% perlite + 15% coarse bark. pH 5.8–6.5. Avoid dense mixes or those with moisture-retaining crystals; refresh top 2–3 cm annually.
- **Fertilize:** Mar–Sep, balanced at half strength. In Jan–Feb switch to bloom booster (10-30-20) at quarter strength to prime spring flowering. Flush soil with plain water every 3 months.
- **Repotting:** every 2 years or when roots mass at drainage holes. Up-pot 2–3 cm only — oversized pots are the most common path to root rot. Best: early spring. Division at repotting is the only propagation method.
- **Propagation:** division only at repotting — tease clump into sections each with 2–3 leaves and their own roots. Leaf and stem cuttings do not root.
- **Toxic to pets:** yes — calcium oxalate crystals cause oral burning, drooling, vomiting; contact vet if ingested. All parts affected, including the spathe and spadix.

## Common issues

- **Brown crispy leaf tips** → low humidity (most common in winter heating season): raise to 50%+. / Fluoride or chlorine accumulation: switch to filtered water and flush soil. / Salt buildup: flush with 3× pot volume of water.
- **Yellowing leaves (multiple, wet soil)** → overwatering: reduce frequency, check drainage; inspect roots for rot. / Natural ageing (one old outer leaf): remove at base, normal.
- **No flowers / failure to rebloom** → insufficient light (most common — needs 3,000+ lux): relocate. / Wrong fertiliser (high-N): switch to bloom booster in late winter. / Missing cool rest at 16–18°C in autumn/winter.
- **Dramatic wilting** → dry soil = thirsty; bottom-soak, recovers in 1–2 hours. / Wet soil = root rot: unpot immediately, cut rotten roots, repot in fresh dry mix.
- **Soft brown spots on leaves** → fungal or bacterial from water on foliage overnight; remove affected leaves, improve airflow, avoid overhead watering.
- **Pale or washed-out new leaves** → too much direct light, or soil pH above 7.0 locking out iron; correct pH to 5.8–6.5.

## Pest playbook

- **Fungus gnats** — most common due to high-moisture soil; small flies near soil, larvae eat roots. Let top 3 cm dry between waterings; BTI drench; yellow sticky traps.
- **Spider mites** — fine webbing, stippled leaves; thrives when humidity drops below 40%. Shower foliage; 70% isopropyl on leaf undersides; repeat every 4 days × 3 cycles.
- **Mealybugs** — white cottony tufts in leaf axils. Isopropyl swab every 5 days × 3 cycles. Check roots at next repotting; systemic drench if root mealybugs found.
- **Scale** — brown bumps on stems. Scrape, then weekly neem oil for a month. Persistent — requires consistency.
- **Thrips** — silvery streaks, black frass. Spinosad every 5 days × 3 weeks; blue sticky traps; treat undersides and new growth.
- **Aphids** — clusters on new growth and spathes. Water jet; insecticidal soap; clears in 2–3 treatments.

## Seasonal calendar

- **Spring (Mar–May):** Resume monthly fertilising. Repot if root-bound. Increase watering cadence. Primary blooming window — new spathes appear if light and phosphorus were adequate over winter.
- **Summer (Jun–Aug):** Peak growth. Full fertilising cadence. Watch humidity (AC dries air). Protect from intense afternoon sun. Flush soil if white salt crust has formed.
- **Autumn (Sep–Nov):** Last fertiliser in September. Reduce watering gradually. Move away from windows that will be cold with winter heating. Good time for soil flush.
- **Winter (Dec–Feb):** Water every 10–14 days; allow top 2–3 cm to dry. No fertiliser. In late January, apply bloom booster at quarter strength. Supplement light if below 1,000 lux most of the day.

## Species-specific notes

*S. wallisii* is the compact Spathiphyllum species (40–60 cm) — verify the cultivar if space is limited, as 'Mauna Loa' and 'Sensation' exceed 120 cm. The wilting behaviour is a deliberate communication mechanism; panic-overwatering in response to every wilt is the most common path to root rot. Fluoride sensitivity is cumulative — brown tips on affected leaves are permanent, but switching to filtered water stops progression on new growth. Bathroom placement often provides ideal humidity and light simultaneously, eliminating the need for a humidifier.
