# Spineless Yucca

*Yucca elephantipes* — easy-tier architectural specimen; the #1 killer is overwatering that rots the swollen cane base before any visible symptoms appear.

## Care at a glance

- **Light:** bright indirect to full sun; best at a south- or west-facing window within 1 m. Min 2,000 lux. Too much: bleached papery patches on sun-facing leaves. Too little: stretched growth, pale colouring.
- **Water:** every 10 days / 21 days winter; soak-and-dry — wait until the top 5 cm is completely dry. Water around the pot perimeter, not into the crown. Tap water is fine.
- **Temperature:** ideal 18–27°C; min survivable 5°C, growth stops below 10°C. Avoid cold windowpane contact and heating vents. Cold damage: translucent patches on leaves, blackening cane base.
- **Humidity:** low, 20–50%. Standard indoor air is perfect. No misting — moisture in the crown causes bacterial rot.
- **Soil:** cactus/succulent mix + 30% perlite. pH 6.0–7.5. Must drain freely; refresh top 2–3 cm annually.
- **Fertilize:** Apr–Sep, balanced (10-10-10) at half strength. None Oct–Mar. Flush soil with plain water every 3 months.
- **Repotting:** every 2 years or when roots circle the base. Up-pot 2–3 cm only; yuccas prefer slightly root-bound. Best: early spring. Use a heavy pot — canes become top-heavy.
- **Propagation:** stem cuttings in spring — cut 15–30 cm cane, callous 24–48 h, plant in dry grit; roots in 3–6 weeks. Offsets: detach basal pups at 15 cm, treat as a cutting.
- **Toxic to pets:** yes — saponins cause vomiting, drooling, and lethargy; seek vet advice if ingested.

## Common issues

- **Yellowing lower leaves (a few at a time)** → natural ageing: remove at base, normal.
- **Yellowing leaves (multiple, wet soil)** → overwatering: let dry fully; unpot to inspect roots. Mushy roots: cut to firm tissue, dust with sulphur, repot in dry mix, withhold water 1 week.
- **Brown crispy tips** → fluoride/chlorine (switch to filtered water) or salt buildup (flush soil). Also low humidity near a heat source.
- **Soft, sunken, dark cane base** → basal rot from chronic overwatering. Cut all rotten tissue, callous 48 h, replant in dry gritty mix.
- **Wilting + dry soil** → underwatering; bottom-soak, recovers in hours.
- **Wilting + wet soil** → root rot; unpot immediately, remove rotten roots, repot in dry mix.
- **Leggy, pale, widely spaced new leaves** → insufficient light; relocate and rotate 90° weekly.
- **White crust on soil surface** → mineral/salt buildup; flush and replace top 2–3 cm of mix.

## Pest playbook

- **Spider mites** — fine webbing at cane junctions, stippled leaves; thrives in hot dry air. 70% isopropyl on cotton to affected areas; repeat every 4 days × 3 cycles. Raise humidity.
- **Scale** — hard brown bumps on cane surfaces. Scrape with toothbrush, then weekly isopropyl; severe: imidacloprid drench.
- **Mealybugs** — white tufts in cane axils. Isopropyl swab; heavy: imidacloprid drench.
- **Fungus gnats** — flies near soil; let top 3–5 cm dry fully; BTI drench; yellow sticky traps.
- **Thrips** — silvery streaks, black frass. Spinosad every 5 days × 3 weeks; blue sticky traps.
- **Aphids** — clusters on new growth. Strong water jet; insecticidal soap.

## Seasonal calendar

- **Spring (Mar–May):** Resume watering and fertilising. Best window for repotting and stem cuttings.
- **Summer (Jun–Aug):** Water every 7–10 days by pot weight. Can move outdoors once nights are above 12°C — acclimatise over 2 weeks. Watch for spider mites.
- **Autumn (Sep–Nov):** Taper watering; stop fertilising after September. Bring indoors before nights drop below 10°C.
- **Winter (Dec–Feb):** Water every 18–21 days or when pot feels very light. No fertiliser. Growth stalling is normal.

## Species-specific notes

The swollen cane base stores water and starch — drought resilience is high, but this same tissue stays wet from the inside out even after the surface dries, making it uniquely vulnerable to rot. Multiple canes sold in one pot are separate plants; if one rots, remove it without disturbing the others. Unlike *Y. aloifolia*, the "spineless" variety has no terminal leaf spine, making it safe around children and pets barring ingestion.
