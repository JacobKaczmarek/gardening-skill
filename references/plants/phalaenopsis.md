# Phalaenopsis (Moth Orchid)

*Phalaenopsis hybrid* — beginner-friendly epiphytic orchid; rewards patience with spikes lasting 2–5 months, but requires open-air roots, minimal watering, and a cool autumn rest to rebloom.

## Care at a glance

- **Light:** medium indirect light — 1,000–2,000 lux; bright east or west window, or 1–2 m from south. Min 1,000 lux. Too much: bleached or scorched patches on upper leaf surface. Too little: deep green floppy leaves, no bloom spike after 12+ months.
- **Water:** every 10 days / 14 days winter; soak pot in water 15–20 min, then drain completely — never let roots sit in standing water. Root colour through clear pot: bright green = just watered, silver-white = ready to water. Filtered or rainwater preferred (fluoride/salts accumulate in bark).
- **Temperature:** ideal 18–27°C; min survivable 10°C. Avoid AC vents, cold windows, draughty doors. Bloom trigger: 4–6 weeks of 13–16°C nights in autumn while keeping daytime above 18°C.
- **Humidity:** high, 50–70%. Pebble tray, grouping, or humidifier. Do not mist the crown or open flowers — causes crown rot and petal spotting.
- **Soil:** open orchid bark medium (70% fir bark + 20% perlite + 10% sphagnum). pH 5.5–6.5. Never use standard potting mix. Repot when bark breaks down into a dense, soggy mass (~2 years).
- **Fertilize:** Mar–Oct, "weakly weekly" — balanced orchid fertiliser at quarter strength. Switch to bloom booster (10-30-20) in September–October. No feeding Nov–Feb. Flush bark with plain water every third watering to prevent salt buildup.
- **Repotting:** every 1–2 years when bark is soft/compacted or roots are circling. Always use a clear plastic pot — roots photosynthesize and colour indicates hydration. Up-pot 2–3 cm only. Best: after a bloom cycle ends in spring.
- **Propagation:** keiki only — Phalaenopsis are monopodial (no division). Wait until keiki has 3–4 roots of 3–5 cm before detaching; pot in damp sphagnum. First bloom in 1–2 years.
- **Toxic to pets:** no — non-toxic per ASPCA.

## Common issues

- **Yellow lower leaf (one at a time)** → natural senescence: remove at base, normal.
- **Multiple yellow leaves** → root rot (wet compacted bark): unpot, cut brown/mushy roots, repot in fresh bark. / Prolonged direct sun or cold below 15°C.
- **Wrinkled, accordion-pleated leaves** → chronic underwatering or root loss; soak thoroughly and rebuild roots in moist sphagnum.
- **Bud blast (buds drop before opening)** → cold draught or temperature swing >5°C (most common). / Low humidity or ethylene from nearby fruit. Keep stable from spike emergence through bloom end.
- **No rebloom after 12+ months** → insufficient light (most common — needs 1,500+ lux). / Missing cool-night trigger in autumn. / High-nitrogen feed keeping plant vegetative — switch to bloom booster.
- **Brown crispy root tips** → salt/fertiliser buildup or hard tap water; flush bark, switch to filtered water.
- **Crown rot (mushy, foul-smelling centre)** → water pooled in crown overnight. Remove affected tissue, apply 3% hydrogen peroxide, allow to dry; water in mornings only.

## Pest playbook

- **Scale** — most common orchid pest; brown oval bumps on leaves and stems. Scrape, then 70% isopropyl weekly; neem oil for 4–6 weeks; severe: imidacloprid.
- **Mealybugs** — white cottony tufts in leaf axils and bark. Isopropyl swab every 5 days × 3 cycles. Check roots for root mealybugs; treat with systemic drench.
- **Spider mites** — fine webbing under leaves, silvery stippling; thrives below 40% humidity. Shower plant; 70% isopropyl to leaf undersides; repeat every 4 days × 3 cycles.
- **Fungus gnats** — flies around bark; larvae eat root tips. Allow bark surface to dry; BTI drench; yellow sticky traps.
- **Thrips** — silver streaks, black frass dots. Spinosad every 5 days × 3 weeks; blue sticky traps.
- **Aphids** — clusters on spike buds; less common. Water jet; insecticidal soap.

## Seasonal calendar

- **Spring (Mar–May):** Repot after bloom if bark has degraded. Resume quarter-strength weekly fertiliser. Primary growth window — expect 1–2 new leaves. Best time to detach keikis.
- **Summer (Jun–Aug):** Peak growth. Maintain feeding cadence. Monitor humidity (AC dries air). Keep out of direct afternoon sun. Aerial roots grow actively — do not trim.
- **Autumn (Sep–Nov):** Bloom-trigger window — provide 4–6 weeks of 13–16°C nights; switch to bloom booster fertiliser. A new spike should emerge from a leaf axil.
- **Winter (Dec–Feb):** Water every 12–14 days. No fertiliser. Keep stable — no relocation once a spike is visible. Blooms typically open January–March and last 2–5 months.

## Species-specific notes

Aerial roots growing outside the pot are normal and photosynthetically active — never bury or trim healthy ones. When a spike finishes blooming, cutting above the second node may yield a secondary flush; cutting at the base conserves energy for a stronger rebloom next cycle. Root colour is the primary hydration indicator: silver-white means water now; bright green means wait.
