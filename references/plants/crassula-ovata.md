# Jade Plant

*Crassula ovata* — beginner-friendly succulent tree that lives for decades; the #1 killer is overwatering combined with low light, causing root rot before visible symptoms appear.

## Care at a glance

- **Light:** bright indirect to some direct sun; best at a south- or west-facing window. Min 2,500 lux. Too much (sudden direct midday sun): brown scorch patches. Too little: stretched internodes, leaves spaced far apart.
- **Water:** every 14 days / 28 days winter; soak-and-dry — wait until the top half of soil is dry. Water at the perimeter, not on the trunk or leaves. Tap water is generally fine.
- **Temperature:** ideal 16–24°C; min survivable 5°C, growth slows below 13°C. Avoid cold draughts and frost. Cold damage: translucent, water-soaked leaf spots.
- **Humidity:** low, 25–50%. Dry indoor air is ideal. No misting — it encourages fungal spotting on the fleshy leaves.
- **Soil:** 2 parts potting soil + 1 part perlite + 1 part coarse sand. pH 6.0–7.0. Must drain freely; refresh top 2–3 cm annually.
- **Fertilize:** Apr–Sep, balanced (5-5-5 or 10-10-10) at half strength. None Oct–Mar. Flush soil with plain water every 3 months.
- **Repotting:** every 2–3 years or when top-heavy. Up-pot 2–3 cm only; jade tolerates being snug. Best: early spring. Use terracotta for stability.
- **Propagation:** stem cuttings most reliable — cut 5–10 cm tip, callous 24–48 h, plant in dry cactus grit; roots in 2–4 weeks. Leaf cuttings are slower and lower success rate.
- **Toxic to pets:** yes — causes lethargy, vomiting, and incoordination; seek vet advice if ingested.

## Common issues

- **Yellowing leaves (multiple, wet soil)** → overwatering: let dry fully; check roots for rot. Mushy roots: cut, dust with sulphur, repot in dry mix, withhold water 1 week.
- **Yellowing leaves (older, a few at a time)** → natural ageing: remove at base, normal.
- **Soft, wrinkled, shrivelled leaves** → underwatering (dry soil): water deeply, plumps in 1–2 days. / Root rot (wet soil): roots can't deliver water even if soil is moist — unpot and inspect.
- **Sudden leaf drop** → cold draught, temperature shock, or repotting stress; stabilise conditions.
- **Brown scorch patches on leaves** → direct midday sun, especially after moving from lower light; acclimatise over 2–3 weeks.
- **Reddish leaf margins** → light stress or cool temps; aesthetic in moderation, stress indicator if severe.
- **Leggy, stretched growth** → insufficient light; relocate and prune for shape — stretched stems won't compact back.
- **White crusty deposits on leaves or soil** → mineral/salt buildup; wipe leaves with damp cloth, flush soil thoroughly.

## Pest playbook

- **Mealybugs** — most common jade pest; white cottony tufts in leaf axils and branch forks. 70% isopropyl swab per colony every 5 days × 3 cycles. Root mealybugs: imidacloprid drench.
- **Spider mites** — fine webbing at branch junctions, stippled leaves. 70% isopropyl on cotton; repeat every 4 days × 3 cycles.
- **Scale** — flat brown bumps on stems. Scrape with toothbrush; weekly neem oil for 4–6 weeks; severe: imidacloprid.
- **Fungus gnats** — flies near soil; easy to prevent by letting top 3–5 cm dry between waterings. BTI drench for larvae.
- **Thrips** — silvery streaks, black frass. Spinosad every 5 days × 3 weeks; blue sticky traps.
- **Aphids** — clusters on new growth. Water jet; insecticidal soap.

## Seasonal calendar

- **Spring (Mar–May):** Resume fertilising; best window for repotting and stem cuttings. Increase watering as growth restarts.
- **Summer (Jun–Aug):** Full growth — water every 10–14 days by pot weight. Can move outdoors to dappled shade; acclimatise over 2 weeks. Continue monthly feeding.
- **Autumn (Sep–Nov):** Taper watering and fertiliser. Last feed in September. Move indoors before first frost. Cool nights (10–13°C) can trigger flowering on mature plants.
- **Winter (Dec–Feb):** Water every 21–28 days or at first sign of leaf softening. No fertiliser. Maintain brightest available spot — light is more limiting than temperature in winter.

## Species-specific notes

Jade is extremely long-lived and can be trained as a bonsai — it responds well to pruning and produces reliable back-budding in spring. Many cultivars exist ('Gollum', 'Hobbit', 'Hummel's Sunset') but all share identical care. A neglected jade that has lost all leaves to drought can often be rescued if green tissue still shows in the trunk: prune dead stems, water cautiously, and wait weeks for dormant buds to break.
